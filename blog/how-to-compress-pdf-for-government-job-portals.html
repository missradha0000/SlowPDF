How to Compress PDFs Under 200KB for Government & Job Portals

Struggling with strict 200KB or 100KB file upload limits on government application forms, state portals, or university admissions? Learn how to optimize document stream filters without destroying text sharpness.

Quick Solution Summary

  • • Government portals strictly reject files over 200KB (or 50KB for signatures/photo proofs).
  • • Excessive file size is usually caused by uncompressed high-DPI image scans or embedded full font subsets.
  • • SlowPDF's in-browser compressor resamples image streams locally, stripping duplicate font definitions while preserving readable text.

Why Government Portals Enforce Strict File Limits

National recruitment boards, state public service commissions, and passport portals receive millions of applications during peak registration windows. To prevent server storage overload and slow database queries, portal legacy architectures enforce rigid file size ceilings—frequently 200KB max for degree certificates and 50KB for photo signatures.

3 Technical Reasons Your PDF Is Oversized

  1. Uncompressed JPEG Scans: Phone scanner apps frequently export 300 DPI or 600 DPI images directly into PDF containers, yielding 5MB–10MB files.
  2. Embedded TrueType Fonts: Word processors embed full font tables containing thousands of unused glyphs inside every saved PDF.
  3. Unnecessary Metadata Streams: Photoshop and Acrobat inject thumbnail preview caches and author tracking metadata.
